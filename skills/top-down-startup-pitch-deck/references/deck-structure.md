# Deck structure

Two sequences share one design system. The investor deck is the default.

Titles below are **roles**. On the actual slide, the small section label shows the role ("03  THE MARKET") and the headline is a full-sentence takeaway specific to the company.

## Investor deck (default, 9–11 slides)

| # | Label | Job of the slide | Content | Sourcing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | Cover | Who you are and what you want, in one glance | Logo, name, one-line tagline, subline with the ask (e.g. "Raising $750K pre-seed to reach 100 paying clinics"), company, city, contact | Company data |
| 01 | The Tagline | The answer: what you do, for whom, why it matters | A 5–8 word statement; 2–3 lines expanding it; one proof chip (a real, checkable proof point) | Proof chip must be verifiable |
| 02 | Founding Team | Why this team wins this market | 2–4 people: name, role, the one credential that matters; or 3 numbered proof points about the team | Company data; public credentials may be linked |
| 03 | The Market | The prize is large and reachable | 3 figures: TAM (top-down, cited), SAM and SOM (bottom-up, arithmetic in notes); one line on the segment you start with | **Every figure cited**; show calculation for SAM/SOM |
| 04 | Pain Points | The problem is real, frequent and costly | 3 stats, each with a bold claim and a one-line consequence | **Every stat cited** |
| 05 | The Solution | What you built, in specifics | 3–5 features written as outcomes with numbers (e.g. "60 s from web form to call-back") | Product figures = company data; benchmarks cited |
| 06 | Why Now | Why this could not have worked 3 years ago and won't wait 3 more | 3 forces (technology cost curve, regulation, adoption/infrastructure) with a dated figure each | **Every force cited** |
| 07 | Unit Economics | How you make money, and that it scales | Pricing tiers, gross margin, CAC, LTV or payback; assumptions listed | Company data; industry benchmarks cited |
| 08* | Traction | Proof it is working | Revenue/users/pilots over time, named customers only with permission | Company data |
| 09* | Competition | Why you win against the alternatives | 2x2 or comparison table on the criteria buyers use | Competitor facts cited, dated |
| 10 | The Ask | What the money does | Amount, instrument if known, use of funds (3–4 buckets with %), milestones it reaches, timeline | Company data |
| — | Sources | Everything above can be checked | Numbered full citations: publisher, title, date, URL, accessed date | — |

*Optional. Include Traction whenever there is any real traction; include Competition when the audience will ask.

Order logic: the answer (Cover, Tagline) first, then why this team, why this market, why this problem, why this solution, why now, why it makes money, then the ask. This is the top-down order: each slide answers the question the previous one raises.

## Product deck (prospects and customers, 8–9 slides)

| # | Label | Content | Sourcing |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Cover | Product name, one-line promise, company | — |
| 01 | The Problem | 3 cited stats about the buyer's pain, each with its consequence for the buyer | **Cited** |
| 02 | The Solution | What the product does, plus 4–5 proof numbers | Company data |
| 03 | How It Works | 3 steps (trigger, action, result), with integrations named | — |
| 04 | What You Get | 4–6 features as outcomes | — |
| 05 | Who It's For | 3–4 verticals or roles, each with its specific use | — |
| 06 | Pricing | Tiers, what is included, overage and terms | Company data |
| 07 | Why Us | Comparison against named alternatives on buyer criteria | **Competitor facts cited and dated** |
| 08 | Get Started | Onboarding steps and timeline, one clear call to action, contact | — |
| — | Sources | Full citations | — |

## Headline rules (every slide)

- A full sentence stating the conclusion, not a label: "[Clinics] lose [X]% of new-patient calls after hours" (figure sourced on the slide) rather than "The Problem".
- Headlines are at most two lines, 12 words where possible.
- Parallel grammar across slides where they form a group.
- If the headline carries a number, the number is sourced on that slide.

## Body rules

- One idea per slide. About 40 words of body text or less; move detail to speaker notes.
- Figures: big number, bold one-line claim, one muted line of consequence.
- Three items beats five; five is the maximum in any list.
- No testimonials unless real and permitted; no customer logos without permission.
