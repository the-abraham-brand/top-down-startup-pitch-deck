# Research, verification and sources

These decks go in front of investors. One figure that is wrong, stale, or traced to a vendor's marketing page costs the credibility of the whole deck. Every external figure must therefore come from a trusted provider **and** pass verification.

There is no fixed list of approved sources. The right sources depend on the startup's industry, the problem it solves and its solution, so choose them per deck using the criteria below.

## Step 1: Plan the research from the solution

Before searching, work backwards from what the deck has to prove:

1. **Solution → problem.** What problem does the product solve, for which customer, in which geography?
2. **Problem → claims.** List the claims each slide needs: how many customers have the problem, how often it happens, what it costs them, how big the spend is, what is changing now, what competitors charge, and the industry benchmarks for the unit economics.
3. **Claims → who would know.** For each claim, identify who produces authoritative data on it in *this* industry and geography: the regulator, the statistics office, the industry body, the leading analysts, established data publishers such as Statista, established news agencies such as Reuters, the peer-reviewed research, or the companies' own filings.

A healthtech deck in the UAE, a fintech deck in India and a logistics deck in Europe will draw on completely different sources. Record the plan (claims and intended sources) in your working notes before you start.

## Step 2: Judge each source against the trust test

A source is trusted for a figure only if it passes all five tests:

| Test | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | It produced the data (official statistics, regulator, filing, study, survey), or it is an established publisher that verifies data and names its underlying source (e.g. Statista, Reuters) | It repeats a number without naming where it came from |
| **Method** | The method is stated or evident: sample, period, definition, or the underlying dataset | No method, no sample, no definition |
| **Independence** | It has no commercial interest in the claim being true | It is a vendor's own "study" promoting the product category it sells, or a paid placement |
| **Currency** | It is dated, and the figure is recent enough for the claim (prefer the last 3 years; older figures are dated on the slide) | Undated, or old enough that the market has clearly moved |
| **Fit** | Same geography, segment, year and definition as the claim on the slide | A US figure supporting an India claim; "conversational AI" supporting "voice agents" |

Sources that usually fail and should not be cited: vendor and agency blogs, "statistics roundup" pages, content-marketing "reports", forums and social posts, AI-generated summaries, undated pages. Use them only as leads to find the original, then cite the original if it passes the test.

Syndicated market-report sellers (which publish TAM forecasts that often disagree widely with each other) need extra care: only cite one when its figure is corroborated by an independent source in Step 3, and label it as a forecast by that publisher.

## Step 3: Verify every figure

For each external figure:

1. **Open the page.** Search snippets are leads, not evidence. The figure must be on the page you cite.
2. **Match exactly.** Same number, unit, currency, geography, year and definition as the slide. If the source differs, change the slide to match the source, not the other way round.
3. **Trace to the origin.** If the page quotes someone else, follow it to the producer of the data and cite that producer too (e.g. "Statista, citing [agency], 2025").
4. **Corroborate headline figures.** Any figure that appears in a slide headline, or that the investment case rests on (market size, core pain statistic, growth rate), must be supported by a second independent trusted source, or by the producer's own primary dataset. If two credible sources disagree, show the more conservative figure or the range, and note both.
5. **Record the verification status** in the ledger: Verified, Corroborated (two sources), Mismatch, or Unverified.

If a figure cannot be verified, it does not go on the slide as a statistic. In order of preference: find a verified replacement, reword the claim without the number, use the company's own data (labelled), or leave `[needs source]` visible and list it for the founder. Never fill the gap with a plausible number, and never cite a source for a figure it does not contain.

## What needs an outside source

| Needs a trusted outside source | Company data (label it; no outside source needed) |
|---|---|
| Market size, growth, number of target customers | The company's own revenue, users, pilots, growth |
| How often the problem occurs and what it costs | The company's own pricing, costs, margins |
| Why-now forces: cost curves, regulation and deadlines, adoption | Product performance the company measured |
| Competitor pricing and features (their official pages, dated) | Team credentials, plans, milestones, use of funds |
| Industry benchmarks (CAC, churn, margins, conversion) | |

Forecasts are labelled as forecasts with the publisher and year ("projected to reach $X by 2030, per Publisher"). Currency conversions state the rate and date in the speaker notes.

## Market sizing

- **TAM**: total spend or number of potential customers for the category, from a trusted, verified source, labelled with year and geography.
- **SAM**: the part you can serve with the current product and channels. Build it: number of target customers (sourced) × annual price (company data).
- **SOM**: what you can realistically win in 3–5 years, built from stated capacity and conversion assumptions, not a flat percentage of TAM.
- Show the arithmetic in the speaker notes. Never present TAM as revenue you will capture.

(Illustrative format. Every real row must come from a page you opened.)

## On the slide

- A small bracketed number next to each sourced figure, matching the ledger.
- A one-line footer: "Sources: [1] Publisher, year; [2] …" in the smallest type size.
- Company figures get the footer "Company data, [month year]".
- The final Sources slide lists every citation in full: number, publisher, title, underlying origin, date, URL, accessed date.
